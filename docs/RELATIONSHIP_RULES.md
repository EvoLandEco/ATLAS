# Links between reports, places and events

ATLAS maps source-described travel, shared events and epidemiological hypotheses. The source network shows which documents cover each reporting topic. The [technical review](LINK_REVIEW.md) explains the research basis and the fit to the available data.

## Geographic links

Every geographic link has a type, two named endpoints, location precision, a source assessment, a limitation and at least one supporting quotation. The quotation must support the relationship and its named places, as well as any direction or certainty shown.

| Type | Rule | Display |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Travel or importation | A source explicitly describes travel, importation or medical transfer between two named places. | Blue line with an arrow in the reported direction. |
| Shared event | A source links cases in both places to the same named gathering or exposure event. | Green line without an arrow. |
| Source hypothesis | A source proposes an epidemiological connection or common introduction mechanism and names both places. | Dashed brown line, with the source's uncertainty in the label and panel. |

Travel arrows describe movement. Shared-event links describe a common association. Hypothesis lines preserve the source's proposed explanation. The panel provides the source quotation, dates and a question or limitation relevant to investigation.

Country and regional reference points retain their stated precision. A contextual endpoint may appear when a source names a place without supplying a separate report about it. Keep that endpoint separate from unrelated diseases reported in the same country. For example, the South Ubangi cholera context and DRC Bundibugyo reporting are separate topics even when both use a DRC reference point.

## Source assessments

The evidence panel also covers outbreak follow-up, transmission, exposure and unresolved questions. A source must identify the cases, hosts, episode or setting involved. Retain reported uncertainty, negative tests, competing explanations and later assessments. Where geographic endpoints are unavailable, use a labelled diagram of the people, hosts or reports involved.

For outbreak follow-up, compare pathogen, population, place, episode and case scope. An explicit follow-up or authority outbreak identifier can support continuity. Publisher article IDs identify documents. Record conflicting measurements within an episode and refer unresolved identity questions for review.

## Reporting and comparison

Shared disease groups help readers find comparable material. Shared documents connect sources to the topics they cover. These are displayed as topic metadata and a source–topic network. They do not generate geographic links.

Nearby coordinates, overlapping publication dates, similar totals, or matching broad disease names alone do not supply a relationship. Any proposed statistical linkage method needs its own inputs, assumptions and evaluation. Publication dates remain reporting dates; country reference points remain map locations.

## Time window

Both slider handles and the date fields select an inclusive interval. Publication mode selects publication or edition dates; capture mode selects source collection dates. The same window applies to map topics, source links, the chronology and source assessments. All supporting report entries must fall inside it. Later assessments enter with their own supporting reports.

Playback moves the selected window forward at a fixed width. The window describes when reports appeared or were collected. Observation periods and travel dates remain in the source text.

## Reassessment

Use the [link reassessment procedure](LINK_REASSESSMENT.md) when evidence, rules or editorial decisions change. Reuse unchanged source assessments and review the historical relationships that new evidence can affect. Explicit references and investigation identifiers select candidates across the archive; publication proximity and common disease names do not establish a connection. Preserve competing statements and review history.

## Editorial decisions and language

Source annotations provide leads for investigation. An editor accepts, rejects or defers epidemiological relationships after reviewing the evidence. Event merges, splits and corrections require recorded decisions. Map links remain outside the accepted event registry.

Use direct labels such as “Reported importation”, “Shared festival” and “Possible cross-border link”. State what connects the places and preserve the source's degree of certainty. Keep the main view concise and put detailed methods in the technical review. The [workflow guide](WORKFLOW.md) covers language for docs and interface text.
